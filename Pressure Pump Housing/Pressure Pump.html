Reverse Engineering & Structural Optimization

Pressure Pump Intermediate Housing and Motor Mount

Reverse-engineered intermediate drive housing bridging the electric motor and high-pressure manifold head. Digitized from physical measurements, then redesigned and structurally optimized in SolidWorks to improve fatigue life, reduce stress concentrations, and prevent high-pressure flange distortion.

Engineering and Design Overview

Process and Methodology Precision caliper measurement, pilot register tolerancing, parametric CAD reconstruction.
Functional Interfaces Direct rear electric motor flange register, front high-pressure head mounting, and integrated wiring junction box.
Life-Cycle Optimization Reinforced structural bolt columns, stress-relief corner transitions, and uniform wall thickness for fatigue resistance.

Technical Model and Design Improvements

FIGURE / 01 Parametrically Remastered and Optimized Housing

The housing serves as the critical structural link between the drive motor and high-pressure fluid pump head. The legacy part was measured and converted into a native parametric SolidWorks model with targeted fatigue enhancements.

  • Axial Bolt Columns: Continuous reinforced pillars supporting the front pressure head mounts, counteracting cyclic hydraulic pressure pulses that cause flange deflection and seal leaks.
  • Integrated Terminal Enclosure: Low-profile side junction box featuring continuous perimeter wall sealing and dedicated corner fastener posts.
  • Radius Blending: Generous internal fillets replace sharp shoulder corners to eliminate notch-stress concentrations under vibration.

FIGURE / 02 Motor Mount Pilot Face and Alignment Registers

Detailed view of the base mounting geometry designed for concentric alignment between the electric motor shaft and pump impeller drive.

  • Pilot Register Tolerancing: Precise circular centering step engineered to prevent angular misalignment, bearing runout, and mechanical seal degradation.
  • Flange Ribbing: Four radial mounting lugs distributed evenly along the base to anchor motor drive reaction torque securely.
